Honest evaluation before accepting paying customers · Generated for the Dojo Roleplay engineering and operations team

Bottom line: The product is real, the architecture is sound, and the foundations are right. You can ship to 1–2 friendly customers tomorrow if you go in eyes-open. Do not open this up to a list of 10 paying clients yet.

The remaining gaps are operational, not architectural — backups, monitoring, legal, support routing. Each is small in isolation; together they're the difference between "MVP" and "I'm comfortable charging strangers."

What's genuinely good

The product itself works

A school admin can sign up, invite staff, watch them practice calls, see scorecards with detailed feedback, and review usage. The roleplay quality with the Vapi-side LLMs and the scoring rubrics is the actual product — that exists and works. Most "MVP SaaS at this stage" don't have anything close to this much functionality.

Multi-tenancy and architecture

Multi-tenancy with school_id is enforced. Global admins are properly null-school. Auth flows through Supabase. The school switcher gives a clean operating model. Per-page filters live in the right places — analytical pages have their own scope, operational pages use the sidebar. These are decisions a lot of teams get wrong and have to refactor later.

Cost tracking and caps

Vapi's end-of-call-report is captured. OpenAI scoring tokens are captured. Markup is applied at the read boundary. Caps enforce at three places (web session-token, phone webhook, UI guard). When a school hits its cap, calls genuinely stop — verified end-to-end. This is the single most important production-readiness item for a usage-based SaaS, and it is in place.

Audit logging

The phone_call_attempts table records every inbound call with outcome (accepted, rejected_unknown, rejected_rate_limit, rejected_cap). calls, scorecards, and users are properly relationally linked. If a customer disputes a charge, the receipts are there.

Risks to fix before clients (blockers)

Blocker 1. Backup recovery is unverified

Supabase has automatic daily backups on paid plans, but a backup that hasn't been restored is not a backup. Until a real restore is performed, the worst-case recovery story is unknown.

  • What to do: Clone the production DB to a staging Supabase project. "Delete" some data. Restore from backup. Confirm it returns. ~30 minutes once.
  • Why it matters: If a customer's data is lost and you can't restore it, that's a refund, a customer lost, and possible legal exposure.

Blocker 2. No uptime monitoring

Render outages, Supabase outages, or backend crashes go undetected until a customer emails. There is no alerting layer.

  • What to do: UptimeRobot's free tier on a backend endpoint that touches the DB (e.g. /api/auth/me). 5-minute check, email alert. Free, 5 minutes to set up.
  • Why it matters: Customers will notice an outage before you do, which is a worst-case operational posture.

Blocker 3. Privacy / Terms of Service / call-recording consent

The platform records phone calls and stores transcripts. In California, Florida, Pennsylvania, Washington, and roughly nine other US states, two-party consent laws apply — disclosure is legally required.

  • Verify: The Vapi assistant's first message includes "this call may be recorded."
  • Required documents: Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, call-recording consent clause.
  • What to do: Lawyer-reviewed templates, $300–500. Non-negotiable before signing customers.

Blocker 4. The Vapi inbound phone whitelist has not been tested live

The diagnosis (misconfigured Server URL on the Vapi phone number) is identified. The code is correct. Until a real call has been made to verify both the whitelist rejection AND the cap-rejection paths, this is theoretical.

  • What to do: Make a real call from a registered phone number AND from an unregistered number. Confirm the rejection messages play correctly. Verify phone_call_attempts rows.
  • Why it matters: A misconfigured Vapi number can silently bleed real money to anyone curious enough to dial it.

Important 5. No "delete a school's data" admin action

If a customer cancels and asks for data deletion (a real GDPR/CCPA right in many jurisdictions), the only path today is manual SQL. The deleteSchool DB helper exists but may not be wired to a UI button.

  • What to do: Verify or add a delete-school button to /admin/schools. ~30 minutes of work.

Important 6. No customer support routing

Where does a school admin go when they can't log in? Today: nowhere. The only escalation is calling or emailing the founder directly. Fine for 1–2 friendly customers; chaotic for 10.

  • What to do: A support@yourdomain.com mailto link in the user dropdown, on error pages, and in the cap-reached banner. Set up a real inbox that someone monitors.

Risks worth addressing soon, but not blocking customer #1

Later 7. Test coverage is weak

52 server tests pass, but they focus on scoring rubrics and a few API behaviors. There is no end-to-end test that exercises the money path: user makes a call → stored → scorecard generates → usage updates → cap enforces. Two test files (scoring.test.js, credentials.test.js) have parse errors and are not running. Frontend has no tests at all.

  • Mitigation: Stay close to the first 3–5 customers, watch logs daily, treat "weird" reports as serious until disproven. Add tests on the money path before scaling.

Later 8. No staging environment

Deploys go directly to production. A bug in production is a bug in a customer's face. A Render preview environment plus a staging Supabase project takes ~30 minutes to set up.

Later 9. AI-generated code review burden

A significant portion of the codebase was AI-generated and spot-reviewed. Subtle bugs are likely lurking, most plausibly in:

  • Cap math in edge cases (race conditions when calls land during a boundary check).
  • School switcher's interaction with cached queries (two such bugs already found and fixed; more may exist).
  • The Vapi webhook handler — long, branchy, hard to trace.

Mitigation: Watch the first customers' logs closely. Set up daily review of phone_call_attempts outcomes and any failed scoring runs.

Later 10. No incident response plan

If OpenAI or Vapi has an outage, scoring fails silently — calls land with status: completed but no scorecard. There is no automated retry mechanism. Manageable for 5 customers; painful at 50.

Later 11. No customer onboarding documentation

When the first paying customer signs, what do they receive? "How to log in, invite staff, run a first call, read a scorecard" — a simple one-pager or a 5-minute Loom is the difference between a customer using the product on day 1 and emailing four questions before giving up.

Recommended go/no-go thresholds

For 1–2 friendly customers (people who'll forgive bugs, ideally martial arts schools with an existing relationship): can ship today. Items 1, 2, 3, 4 are non-negotiable but each is a few hours of work. Items 5 and 6 should be done within a week of the first signed customer.

For 5–10 paying customers (people who paid expecting a working product): items 1–6 all done. Item 11 (onboarding doc) ready. Items 7–9 in flight.

For self-serve sign-up (any credit card, any time): items 1–11 done. Plus Stripe billing automation, "delete account" button, public marketing site, signed Terms of Service flow, and support email infrastructure. This is a different product stage — months out.

The most important single next step

Run a real test call from an unregistered phone number through the cap-reached path, today.

This single test validates that your most important defensive layer — the budget protection that prevents a Vapi misconfiguration from bleeding $50/day while you sleep — actually works in production. Everything else is fixable. A silent money leak is the one item that should never reach a paying customer.
